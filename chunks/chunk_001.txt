Permanent Establishment 3.1 Permanent establishment (PE) in domestic law and tax treaties Refer to the definition in s 6(1) of the 1936 Act – consider in detail what is included, and what might not be included, in this definition. The general position in Australia is that a non-resident includes in their assessable income all income from sources in Australia (refer s6-5(3) of the 1997 Act), irrespective of whether there is a PE as defined in s 6(1) of the 1936 Act. Where a tax treaty applies and the non-resident does not have a PE in Australia (as defined in the treaty), Australia cannot tax any business profits, even if the income is from sources in Australia. The PE definition in s 6(1) of the 1936 Act is relevant to the WHT provisions (refer s 128B of the 1936 Act). Consider Article 5 of the Australia/Germany tax treaty; compare to Article 5 of the UK tax treaty, Article 5 of the US tax treaty and Article 4 of the Singapore tax treaty. The MLI and BEPS Actions recommended an expansion of the tax treaty definition of PE to address the situation of an offshore company selling goods or services into a jurisdiction in such a way that there is no physical presence and no PE (refer Art 5(5), 2017 OECD Model Convention, and Article 5(8) of Australia/Germany tax treaty). In general, for such an important concept, there is surprisingly little case law on PE in Australia. Consider: • *Unisys Corporation Inc v FCT 2002 ATC 5146 • *McDermott Industries (Aust) Pty Ltd [2005] FCFCA 67 and • TR 2002/5 (permanent establishment) (latest consolidated version). While not a tax case, the recent privacy law case about Facebook is interesting, because of what it says about whether Facebook is ‘carrying on business’ in Australia where this business is ‘not manifested in physical or material matter or structures or goods’ (per Allsop CJ, [3]). • *Facebook Inc v Australian Information Commissioner [2022] FCAFC 9 3.2 Multinational Anti-Avoidance Law and Diverted Profits Tax Australia has enacted the Multinational Anti-Avoidance Rule (MAAL) to address the avoidance of a permanent establishment by large global multinational enterprises selling services or goods into Australia. The MAAL is in Part IVA of the 1936 Act (the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, or GAAR): s 177DA of the 1936 Act (and related provisions of the GAAR). The MAAL is intended to address, to some extent, the “avoidance” of PE or taxable status in Australia where there are marketing/sales activities into Australia. It requires a principal purpose of obtaining a tax benefit of not being taxed in Australia. Australia subsequently enacted the Diverted Profits Tax (DPT) in Part IVA of the 1936 Act, ss 177H – 177R and in Diverted Profits Tax Act 2017.
