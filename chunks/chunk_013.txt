For the purposes of s 855-30(2) if the assets of an entity, called “the first entity”, include membership interests in a lower entity, called “the other entity”, the membership interests in “the other entity’ are treated as two assets: • assets that are taxable Australian real property (the TARP assets) (s 855-30(3)(a)), and • assets that are not taxable Australian real property (the non-TARP assets) (s 855-30(3)(b)). The market value of the TARP and non-TARP of the “other entity” must then be determined (s 855-30(4). If a membership interest satisfies the non-portfolio interest test and the principal asset tests, the foreign resident’s entire membership interest is subject to Australian CGT. Under the measures once the test is satisfied the entire gain is subject to Australian taxation even though all or part of the underlying gain may be attributable to assets that are not taxable Australian real property. Note that following the Lamesa decision, s 3A, Agreements Act deems alienation of interests in companies indirectly owning Australian real property to be an alienation of real property in Australia, for the purposes of certain of Australia’s tax treaties. Measures to prevent double counting under the principal asset test In 2014, s 855-32 was enacted with the purpose of preventing the double counting of the market value of assets of a corporate group that are non-TARP (s 855-32(1)(a)) and are created under arrangements under which “corresponding liabilities” are created in other members of the group (s 855-32(1)(b)). Assets of a permanent establishment A foreign resident will be subject to Australian CGT if a CGT event happens to assets used in the foreign resident’s Australian permanent establishment (s 855-15 item 3). The term “permanent establishment article” is defined in s 995-1(1) as having the meaning given by s 855-16. Section 855-16 defines the term “permanent establishment article” as Article 5 of the UK tax treaty within the meaning of the International Tax Agreements Act 1953 (s 855-16(a)) or a corresponding provision of another “international tax agreement” (s 855-16(b)). For situations in which a tax treaty is not applicable, the definition of permanent establishment in s 995-1(1) is used. This provision uses the definition of permanent establishment in s 6(1) of the 1936 Act. An apportionment measure applies if an asset was not exclusively used by a foreign resident in its Australian permanent establishment (s 855-35(2) and (3)).
